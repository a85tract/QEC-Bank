Gauge group, center, and subsystem qubit count

This page derives the static rectangular presentation exported by constructor.py. It uses sign-free Pauli algebra; phases do not enter any rank or commutation statement.

Coordinate and Pauli convention

Let rows be indexed by i=0,...,r-1 and columns by j=0,...,c-1. The row-major qubit index is

$$ q(i,j)=ic+j. $$

The local gauge generators are

$$g^X_{i,j}=X_{i,j}X_{i+1,j},\qquad 0\le i<r-1,\ 0\le j<c,$$

and

$$g^Z_{i,j}=Z_{i,j}Z_{i,j+1},\qquad 0\le i<r,\ 0\le j<c-1.$$

Thus X edges are vertical and Z edges are horizontal. The convention is deliberate: it makes a bare X logical span a row and a bare Z logical span a column. Transposing the grid and exchanging X/Z is equivalent, but it swaps the sector labels used below.

Why the gauge group is nonabelian

Two generators of the same Pauli type commute. A vertical X edge and a horizontal Z edge anticommute exactly when they share one endpoint. For the 3 × 3 constructor order,

g^X_{0,0}=X_0X_3

and

g^Z_{0,0}=Z_0Z_1

share only q0, so their symplectic product is one. The tour highlights this pair as gauge items zero and six. This is why the individual pair outcomes cannot all be stabilizer eigenvalues.

Constructing the center

For each adjacent row pair, multiply every vertical X edge across the columns:

$$S^X_i=\prod_{j=0}^{c-1}g^X_{i,j} =\prod_{j=0}^{c-1}X_{i,j}X_{i+1,j}.$$

For each adjacent column pair, multiply every horizontal Z edge across the rows:

$$S^Z_j=\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}g^Z_{i,j} =\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}Z_{i,j}Z_{i,j+1}.$$

An X strip and a Z strip overlap on the four corners of their 2 × 2 intersection, so they commute. More strongly, an X strip meets an individual horizontal Z edge on zero or two sites, and a Z strip meets an individual vertical X edge on zero or two sites. Every strip therefore commutes with the entire gauge group, not merely with the other strips.

The constructor checks both statements:

  • CheckMatrix.validate() rejects any stabilizer/gauge anticommutation;
  • rank([G;S])=rank(G) proves that every declared center row lies in the generated gauge span.

Rank ledger

Within one column, the vertical X supports are the r-1 rows of the open length-r repetition parity matrix. They are independent. Different columns have disjoint supports, so

$$ rank(G_X)=c(r-1). $$

Similarly, the horizontal Z supports form an independent length-c open chain inside each row:

$$ rank(G_Z)=r(c-1). $$

X-only and Z-only symplectic rows occupy different binary halves, so their ranks add:

$$ rank(G)=c(r-1)+r(c-1)=2rc-r-c. $$

The X center contains r-1 independent adjacent-row strips; the Z center contains c-1 independent adjacent-column strips. Hence

$$ rank(S)=r+c-2. $$

For a subsystem code with gauge group G and center stabilizer group S, the protected and gauge dimensions are

$$k=n-\frac{rank(G)+rank(S)}{2},\qquad g=\frac{rank(G)-rank(S)}{2}.$$

Substituting n=rc gives

$$ k=1,\qquad g=rc-r-c+1=(r-1)(c-1). $$

Bare protected Pauli algebra

Choose

$$\bar X_b=\prod_{j=0}^{c-1}X_{0,j},\qquad \bar Z_b=\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}Z_{i,0}.$$

The subscript b means *bare*: each operator commutes with the full gauge group. \bar X_b meets each horizontal Z edge in its row twice, and \bar Z_b meets each vertical X edge in its column twice. They meet each other once at q(0,0), so they anticommute and generate one protected Pauli pair.

A complete X row at height i differs from the row at i+1 by S^X_i. Likewise, neighboring complete Z columns differ by S^Z_j. Parallel bare representatives therefore differ by center elements, which are also gauge elements.

This bare pair proves a protected qubit exists, but it is not yet the distance proof. Distance permits multiplication by noncentral gauge operators and thus minimizes over dressed representatives; that quotient is derived on the distance page.

What the calculation does not establish

  • It does not specify a measurement schedule. Noncommuting pair generators require time ordering and a detector convention.
  • It does not turn the long center strips into local stabilizers. Only their measurement factors are local.
  • It does not prove a scalable distance theorem merely because the shared Atlas referee exactly enumerates the shipped small instances. Certified lower bounds beyond the exact-search ceiling and equivalence-safe changes of subsystem presentation remain routed to wanted:subsystem-dressed-distance.
  • It does not make the compass Hamiltonian self-correcting or topological. Hamiltonian protection is a dynamics claim with separate evidence.
