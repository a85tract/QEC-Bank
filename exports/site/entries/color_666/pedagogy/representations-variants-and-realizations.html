Variant ledger

6.6.6 versus 4.8.8. Both realize the abstract two-dimensional color-code order on trivalent, face-three-colorable cellulations. Their face weights, vertex density, clipped boundaries, extraction circuits, and finite qubit formulas differ. The comparative resource analysis of Bombín and Martín-Delgado supports the relation, not numerical transfer between entries. In particular, the circuit study of Landahl, Anderson, and Rice analyzes a 4.8.8 architecture; its numerical results are context, not 6.6.6 data.

Triangular color boundary versus Pauli boundary. The constructor fixes one red, green, and blue side. A patch whose sides condense Pauli-labelled sectors can have a similar outline and different logical/surgery behavior. The boundary condition belongs in the formal identity.

Seven-qubit Steane block. The distance-three member is locally the same stabilizer code up to row and qubit relabeling. Steane's algebraic presentation and this family's growing geometry answer different questions; fixed-block circuit evidence is not automatically scaling evidence.

Hybrid lattice surgery. Joining 6.6.6 and 4.8.8 regions deliberately changes the local tiling to trade face weight against layout cost. The hybrid device is neither an alias nor an isolated member of either uniform family.

Tensor contraction. Connecting encoder tensors removes or pairs legs and creates a new global stabilizer code. Every resulting n, k, boundary, and distance must be recomputed.

The disciplined use of an equivalence

An equivalence is most useful when it tells the reader which computation to move to another language. Unfolding motivates projected surface-code decoding; anyon condensation organizes boundary joins; the Ising model organizes likelihood correlations; tensor pushing organizes composition. None authorizes copying a distance, threshold, or hardware number without rebuilding the finite identity tuple. That restriction is the difference between a representation dictionary and an accidental conflation of codes.
