Why the square torus gives `[[2L²,2,L]]`

Statement and scope

For integer L >= 2, take an L-by-L square fundamental domain and identify opposite sides. Put one qubit on each horizontal and vertical edge, an X check at each vertex, and a Z check on each face boundary. The result has n=2L², k=2, and d=L [Kitaev 1997; Kitaev 2003; Dennis et al. 2002]. The proof below is for this square cellulation, not an arbitrary torus mesh.

From cells to commuting checks

Let C2, C1, and C0 be the binary spaces generated by faces, edges, and vertices. Define

C2 --d2--> C1 --d1--> C0 .

Each column of d2 marks the four boundary edges of one face. Each column of d1 marks the two endpoints of one edge. At a corner of a face, exactly two of its boundary edges touch that vertex. Therefore every entry of d1 d2 is zero modulo two. The constructor checks this matrix identity directly.

Set HX=d1 and HZ=d2^T. Then

HX HZ^T = d1 d2 = 0,

so every X star commutes with every Z plaquette. In Pauli language a neighboring pair overlaps on two qubits, producing two anticommutation signs; a non-neighboring pair overlaps on none.

Cell count and stabilizer rank

The periodic domain has L² vertices and L² faces. Choose at every vertex the horizontal edge leaving it and the vertical edge leaving it. This counts each edge exactly once, so there are 2L² edge qubits.

Every column of d1 has two ones. Hence the sum of all vertex rows is zero. To see that no further dependency exists, select a spanning tree of the connected periodic graph. Its vertex-edge incidence matrix has rank L²-1: starting at a leaf, its unique incident tree edge forces that leaf coefficient to vanish in any row dependency; removing leaves iteratively leaves only the global all-vertex relation. Thus rank(HX)=L²-1.

Apply the same argument to the connected dual graph. Faces are dual vertices and primal edges are dual edges, so rank(HZ)=L²-1. The total stabilizer rank is 2L²-2, and

k = n - rank(HX) - rank(HZ)
  = 2L² - (L²-1) - (L²-1)
  = 2.

This also identifies the two global dependencies: the product of every star and the product of every plaquette are each identity because every qubit appears twice.

Homology gives the logical operators

A Z string is a binary edge chain. It commutes with all stars exactly when its boundary is zero, so closed Z strings lie in ker(d1). A product of plaquettes is a face boundary in im(d2). Therefore distinct Z logical classes are

H1(torus; GF(2)) = ker(d1) / im(d2).

There are two generators: one winds horizontally and one vertically. The dual cellulation gives two X cycles. Choose each dual cycle to cross its conjugate primal cycle once and the other primal cycle zero times. Odd intersection is the symplectic product, so each selected X/Z pair anticommutes and the cross-pairs commute.

The constructor exports exactly these four supports and refuses them if they fail stabilizer commutation, fall inside the stabilizer row space, or have the wrong four-by-four symplectic product matrix.

Distance upper bound

A straight row of L horizontal edges winds once around the torus and is a nontrivial Z cycle. A straight column of L vertical edges gives the other Z class. Corresponding straight dual loops supply X logicals. Each has weight L, so d <= L.

Distance lower bound

Take a primal cycle with nonzero horizontal winding. Draw the L parallel vertical dual cuts, one between each neighboring pair of vertex columns. A horizontal-winding cycle has odd intersection with every cut. Each primal edge can cross at most one such cut, so the cycle contains at least L edges. A vertical-winding cycle obeys the rotated argument.

If a cycle has zero winding in both directions, it is a face boundary and is not logical. Thus every nontrivial primal logical has weight at least L. Repeating the cut argument on the dual lattice gives the same lower bound for X logicals. Combining bounds yields d=L.

The L=2 cellulation is small but still satisfies the argument: opposite directions join the same two coordinate layers, yet the constructor keeps four distinct incident edge qubits at every check and exact enumeration returns distance two.
