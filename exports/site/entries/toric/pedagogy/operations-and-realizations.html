Which operations are verified, and what did hardware demonstrate?

Constructor-verified logical Paulis

The constructor exports two primal Z cycles and two dual X cycles. For each support it builds a binary symplectic row and verifies three conditions:

  1. the row commutes with every stabilizer;
  2. it is not in the stabilizer row space; and
  3. the four rows have exactly two conjugate odd-intersection pairs.

Applying one string is a logical Pauli. Because its single-qubit factors can be applied in parallel, the physical depth is one when the control hardware allows simultaneous Paulis. In practice a decoded logical Pauli is usually recorded in a classical Pauli frame, avoiding a physical layer.

The support has weight L; “depth one” does not mean “weight one” and does not remove control, calibration, or correlated-fault concerns.

Verified stacked-block CNOT

Align two identical blocks and apply physical CNOT from each control edge to the corresponding target edge. Pauli conjugation acts as

Xc -> Xc Xt,   Xt -> Xt,
Zc -> Zc,      Zt -> Zc Zt.

An X star on the control becomes the product of corresponding stars on both blocks; a target Z plaquette similarly becomes the product on both blocks. The other two stabilizer sectors remain in place, so the joint stabilizer group is preserved. Exactly the same mapping holds for each homological logical pair, implementing CNOT separately on both encoded qubits.

reproduce.py constructs the doubled symplectic stabilizer space, performs this conjugation, checks equality of row spaces, and checks every selected logical generator. This proves the code-space action. It does not prove that a given device can align blocks, suppress transport faults, or decode correlated two-block events.

Claimed single-block Clifford protocol

guernut2024toric proposes and simulates a constant-depth Toric Clifford toolkit combining fold-transversal gates, Dehn twists, and single-shot logical Pauli measurements. These ingredients require geometric pairings, cellulation-deformation schedules, ancillas, and decoding assumptions absent from the static constructor. The card therefore marks the toolkit and logical measurement as {kind: claimed, ref: guernut2024toric}.

bravyi2013classification supplies a boundary rather than an implementation: locality-preserving constant-depth logical gates for two-dimensional topological stabilizer codes are restricted to the Clifford level. A non-Clifford protocol for a broader abelian phase may add twists or enriched structure, as in bauer2025nonclifford; it is not a native operation of the unmodified square Toric stabilizer. T144 consequently adds no bare T gate.

Both rows are demonstrated because the reported protocols ran on hardware. The descriptions remain different because demonstration status does not erase the distinction between state preparation and active error correction.

Reader decision procedure

When encountering a Toric operation or experiment, ask in this order:

  1. Is the statement only a logical symplectic action, or is there a physical protocol?
  2. If physical, what geometry, ancilla, measurement, and decoder resources are assumed?
  3. Is fault tolerance proved, simulated, or measured?
  4. For hardware, was a code state prepared, were syndromes repeated, was an unknown logical state preserved, and was distance scaling established over a declared range?

This sequence prevents a valid algebraic gate from becoming an unsupported hardware claim and prevents a finite experiment from becoming a universal threshold statement.
